# I avoided American media for 30 days

> The video explores the media landscape and the drastic (or not-so-drastic) changes when US-focused content is avoided or removed from one's digital consumption.
> 
> This is not an anti-American movement or anything, but rather an experiment to simply see what the rest of the world really looks like, and what/whose content I turn to as a replacement.
> 
> The scope focuses on removing or ignoring any media that came from the US or from an American citizen. This does not include US-based topics if made from a foreign country.
> 
> There are plenty of cases where it's not clear if a creator or creation is from the US, so they'll be allowed by default until it's adundantly clear that it is from the US. I will be allowed to finish that piece of content but not proceed with any more from the same source or creator.
> 
> This will not apply to non-influencing creations like animated movies and music. Not that it matters much because I rarely watch movies to begin with, and the grand majority of my playlists are from Japanese producers.

## Script

### Intro

No matter where you go, American media is **everywhere**\*. It's not a bad thing by any means, but it begs the question-- is it *really* everywhere? And how would it affect you *personally* if you were to avoid it entirely, *just for a little bit*.

\[On-screen text: "Unless you're in China or smth"\]

So, for the next 30 days, I will be avoiding **all** US-made content to see what would happen.

Let me make myself abundantly clear: I do **not** have anything against American media... for the most part\*. Quite the opposite---you know what this means for me? No "Jschlatt"\*\*, no Ludwig, no Mint, and worst of all, no rewatching Technoblade videos for **30 days**!\*\*\*

Anyway, here are the rules:

**Rule #1:** I must skip a piece of content if I have ample reason to think it's from the US or by a US creator... duh.

But, **rule #2** allows some exceptions, namely: music, *animated* movies & shows, and any content that I **need**, such as tutorials and guides.

I rarely watch movies to begin with, and my playlists are mostly Japanese so it shouldn't matter much, but I put it here because I ain't updating my playlists, alright?

**Rule #3:** Because it's practically impossible to filter forums and online discussions, I **must** avoid them entirely whenever possible, so no Reddit or Twitter for a month---hopefully forever.

**Rule #4:** US-focused topics from international creators are allowed; it'll be interesting to know what the media landscape would look like without America's megaphone.

And lastly, **rule #5**, if the content's origin is unidentifiable explicitly or *implicitly*, I may continue. But, once I know it's from US, I may finish it but I can't return until the 30 days is over.

With that out of the way, let's get this started.
