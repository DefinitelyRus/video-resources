## Alone Together

*Talk about how social media and attention-seeking apps started creeping into your life--how it felt like it became a necessity out of FOMO, and in some cases, they truly were plain necessary for school, work, or family functions.*

### How it started, how it is now

When we got phones for the first time, it rarely came with strict supervision like what we'd expect of parents today, save for maybe our screentime.

At first, phones were just utility and entertainment. I'd go play Clash of Clans on my way home from school and chat my friends until my phone falls onto my face or my parents catch me awake.

But crucially, at school and when going outside, we didn't use to be on our phones the entire time. If anything, they're often deep in our bags or sometimes even left at home. We went and got it only when we *needed* it.

As time goes on, someone adds you to a group chat, you subscribe to your favorite YouTuber, you sign up for Amazon, you install the McDonald's app--all of which are actively trying to grab your attention.

We got so conditioned that nearly 40% of people in our cohort **can't not** look at their phones *while* talking to someone, and almost 30% claims feeling anxious when they're without their phones.\[1\]

### FOMO

\[Visual: Close-up of multiple Steam friends launching Counter-Strike 2 at once.\]

\[Music: The Escapists, Bernas Pilas - Shankton State Pen @ 0:00\]

The thing about FOMO--or "Fear of Missing Out"--is that nobody's actually obligating you to stay engaged--no one, but yourself.

The fear isn't that you'll miss out on a day of fun; it's that nagging feeling that, by missing out by just **one day**, or by not seeing it unfold in real time, you're suddenly a social outcast whenever they bring up what happened that day.

It's like the screen promised that "as long as you look at me, you won't have to miss out!"\*

By the end of it, you just feel kinda exhausted rather than fulfilled.

What's ironic is that Gen-Z overwhelmingly prefers having in-person communication in the workplace, but even the companies themselves keep pushing for digital communication.\[2\]

But we continue on with it anyway. Keeping in touch, for work or for leisure, all the time, whether we like it or not, even if it means have 5 or more different messaging apps at once.

### Lock-in

*Write about how these platforms have made us dependent on them.*

\[Visual: A folder on a phone containing 5+ messaging apps.\]

Heh. Bet you got a folder like this too.

\[Visual: A venn diagram of which messaging platforms use which apps.\]

If you think about it, social media platforms are almost like monopolies on people's social connections. Ever felt like an outcast for being an Android user among your friends using iMessage? Yeah, exactly.

These platforms won't let you send messages between them either. Wanna chat your relatives? Gotta use Facebook Messenger. Your friends and classmates? Well, you gotta get on Discord **and** Instagram, probably more.

Now, that's not entirely a bad thing. For one, these platforms do provide unique features that entice users to stick around. For example, iMessage allows for animojis, Messenger has deep integration with Meta's other services, Signal and Telegram boast improved privacy, and Discord is an entirely different beast with its multi-channel structure.

All of these are very real benefits that platforms provide that each individual user may find more favorable than others. The problem is that you can't send even the most basic of messages between them--that's why this\* folder exists.

## Disconnected but not lonely

\[Music: Wandering in Kaleidoscope @ 4:10\]

*Talk about my experience removing myself from social media or limiting use of it to such a degree that it no longer significantly hampers my productivity. Include info like which apps I considered useful and which ones weren't actually adding any value.*

When I essentially had no phone for an entire week because the battery *had a little much for dinner*, I noticed how little I actually needed to check in.

Suddenly, not everything's so urgent anymore. I began watching shows that's been sitting in my watch list, collecting dust. I started having ideas worth noting down *on a paper notepad*. It felt like I regained control over my own time.

So, following that train of thought: what *else* can I get rid of?

Immediately, anything that can doomscroll is **gone**, save for a few that I still need. And for those, I set an app timer 'cus Amazon doesn't need more than 30 minutes of my time.

Updates from friends and family? Everyone's on Facebook. If not, Google Meet or meet IRL.
News? I can still read so... Google News?
Memes? I'm bombarded with enough memes as it is on Discord, I don't need more.

### Hand-picked

Still, I have too many messaging apps.

Remember that venn diagram from earlier? Most of my contacts have Messenger, Discord, or both, so that immediately got rid of all the other messaging apps.

After all, if someone has both Messenger and Twitter, but they'll only be active on the latter, then perhaps they didn't want to talk to me that badly.

The inverse is also true: since I have someone who I do want to talk to, doesn't use Messenger, and aren't active on Discord, I have no problem downloading Signal just for them.

More notably, I just mute everyone by default now. Usually, that acts as a statement that "you talk too much", but we gotta let go of that sentiment, man! It's nothing personal, I'd just rather not get notifications for things that aren't urgent or important.

The point is, I can still talk to the people I want to talk to, but this time, *when* I want and *where* I want.

Speaking of which, it also gave me a convenient excuse to prune some *expired contacts*. Those 11 strangers who used to like all my shared posts on Facebook probably won't even notice.

## Becoming a tool

*Talk about reclaiming my attention away from my phone and returning it to its original purpose--utility.*

\[Banner: "I'm not a tool!"\]

\[Music: Fontainebleau - Mind's Journey @ 0:45\]

The modern smartphone unified tens, if not hundreds of tools into one, yet separated the people that use them away from the very people they intended to connect with. It was supposed to be *just* a tool. It's about time we do something about that.

The goal isn't to be a digital hermit or whatever--I bet your ass you'll give up on that before the week is over, so let's go with something more reasonable.

Starting with what I did earlier: choose your apps wisely. You don't need every social media platform under the sun; one or two is probably plenty. If you're lucky enough, you might even get away with carrier pigeons.

Same thing with other apps. Do you *really* need 3 different shopping apps? If you need the best deals that badly, you probably shouldn't be shopping in the first place.

Then, notifications. If you see one you'll just swipe away anyway, you can turn it off! Yes, that specific category of notification for that app. Or, if you think that app has nothing of value to tell you, just mute it entirely!

As for picking up that damn phone first thing in the morning... what if you physically can't? Leave it somewhere across the room. When it rings, you gotta get your ass off your bed to turn it off. Since you're already up anyway, ya might as well get started.

What's that? Can't go to bed without your late-night doomscroll? :pleading:

Get off your bed! Go read a book you've already read, then go back to bed. Repeat until dead! Then do something unusual just for the heck of it.
