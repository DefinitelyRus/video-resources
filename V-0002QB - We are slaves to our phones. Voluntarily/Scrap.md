## Alone Together

***(Intro card)***

\[Music: Ben Prunty - Mantis (Battle) @ 0:00\]

### How it started, how it is now

\[Visual: Fast, nostalgic montage of old phone interfaces. A phone screen showing a Clash of Clans base being raided, or a classic Android/iOS home screen from the mid-2010s.\]

Many of us got our phones pretty early on in our lives—maybe in our teens at the latest. Back then, parents didn't really have strict digital supervision tools like they do today. At most, they just told us to go to sleep.

At first, phones were just pure utility and simple entertainment. I'd play Clash of Clans on my way home from school and text my friends until my phone literally fell onto my face in the dark, or my parents caught me awake.

\[Visual: Cut to a shot of a school backpack being thrown onto a bench. A phone is buried deep inside a zippered pocket, completely out of sight.\]

But crucially, when we went to school or went outside, we didn't use to be on our phones the entire time. They stayed deep in our bags, or sometimes we even left them at home. We reached for the phone only when we *needed* it.

The physical world still held our attention by default.

---

\[Visual: A rapid, escalating sequence of notification banners popping up at the top of a modern screen, overlapping each other.\]

As time goes on, the digital world starts claiming territory. Someone adds you to a mandatory group chat. You subscribe to your favorite YouTuber. You sign up for Amazon or Lazada. You install the McDonald’s app to get a discount code.

Every single one of these apps is actively trying to grab your attention. *Don't forget to ring that bell. Turn on push notifications. Allow tracking.*

Slowly, the default state flips. The phone leaves the bag. It enters your pocket. Then, it stays permanently in your hand.

---

Then, you hang out with a group of friends, and the reality hits you: *"Why is everyone on their phones?! Didn't we come here to hang out??"*

Teto:

 "Ugh... it's so hot. Can we get ice cream instead?"

\[Visual: Close up on Rus, literally steaming in annoyance, staring at the top of Teto's head.\]

We aren't actually together anymore. We are just physically occupying the same space while our minds are trapped in different algorithms. We are alone, together.

### FOMO

\[Music: The track shifts slightly, becoming more ambient and tense.\]

\[Visual: Close-up on a phone screen showing an Instagram Story or Discord activity status. "User is playing..." "User is active now."\]

The engine driving this behavior is FOMO—the Fear Of Missing Out. But it’s no longer just about missing a party on a Friday night. The algorithm has manufactured a hyper-localized, 24/7 version of it.

If you aren't looking at the screen, you are missing the real-time evolution of an inside joke in the group chat. You're missing the immediate reaction to a piece of news. You're missing the cultural currency required to participate in the next conversation.

The fear isn't just that you’ll miss the fun; the fear is that if you step away for even a day, you will become socially invisible to the people who matter to you. The screen promises that as long as you keep looking, you belong.

### Lock-in

\[Visual: A clean, corporate diagram or a visual metaphor of high, smooth walls surrounding a user. No text, just icons of major platforms locking doors shut.\]

And that’s exactly how the lock-in secures itself. These platforms didn't just build messaging tools; they built digital monopolies over our real-world relationships.

They made themselves intentionally incompatible with everything else. You can't send a message from Discord to Messenger. You can't view an Instagram update via a standard RSS feed. If you want to talk to your classmates, you *must* use Meta's infrastructure. If you want to talk to your gaming friends, you *must* open Discord.

They took your real, organic human friendships and held them hostage behind their proprietary software interfaces.

With all those lock-ins in place, it's not like you can just leave... right?
